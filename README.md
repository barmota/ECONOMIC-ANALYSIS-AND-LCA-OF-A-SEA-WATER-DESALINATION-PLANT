This project presents a comprehensive techno-economic and environmental evaluation of a seawater reverse osmosis (SWRO) desalination plant, integrating Levelized Cost of Water (LCOW) analysis with cradle-to-grave Life Cycle
Assessment (LCA) . The economic module disaggregates lifecycle costs into CAPEX (35–45% of total, driven by high-pressure pumps, energy recovery devices, membranes, and 
pretreatment) and OPEX (dominated by energy at 10 – 15 kWh/m³ alongside membrane replacement, chemicals, and brine management), yielding typical LCOWs of $0.50–$60/m³ while stress-testing sensitivity to energy 
price volatility, discount rates, capacity utilization, and long payback horizons of 15–25 years. The parallel LCA quantifies hotspots across construction (embodied carbon in steel, concrete, HDPE, and polyamide membranes),
operation (grid-dependent GWP ranging from 3.5–5.0 kg CO₂-eq/m³ on coal-heavy grids versus under 0.5 kg CO₂-eq/m³ on renewables, plus chemical dosing impacts), and end-of-life (hypersaline brine discharge at ~1.5 L per litre
of freshwater, membrane disposal, and decommissioning waste), identifying the power purchase agreement as the single most decisive variable for decarbonization and flagging marine ecotoxicity risks tied to diffuser design 
and outfall dilution performance. Ultimately, the study frames integrated decision-making around multi-objective optimization of competing priorities — since renewable PPAs lower lifecycle emissions but raise near-term
costs, and zero-liquid-discharge brine systems improve ecological outcomes at a 20–40% LCOW premium — guiding plant configuration toward designs that balance affordability, regulatory compliance, and 
sustainability within local resource contexts.
